Race Recap · Round 16 · October 4, 2026

Verstappen Ends His Drought in the Sepang Rain

A 100-minute rain delay, a string of safety cars and a Mercedes power failure. Max finally gets his first win of 2026.

It took most of a season, a relocated Grand Prix and a tropical downpour, but Max Verstappen is a winner again. He took the Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia by 2.3 seconds over Kimi Antonelli, with Lewis Hamilton completing the podium after one of the drives of the season.

It's Verstappen's first win of 2026, Red Bull's first of the year, and the 72nd of his career. His last one came at the end of last season in Abu Dhabi.

Wait, Bahrain in Malaysia?

Yep. The Bahrain round was originally on the calendar for April, got postponed, and was eventually moved to Sepang because of the conflict in the Middle East. That also meant F1 returned to Sepang for the first time in nine years. The track hadn't lost its famous weather.

The start

A tropical storm hit before the race and pushed the start back by about 100 minutes. When things finally got going, the field ran behind the safety car before a standing start, and roughly half the grid gambled on intermediates.

Verstappen started from pole but didn't get away cleanly. Both Mercedes went past him, with Antonelli leading into Turn 1 and George Russell slotting into second. Hamilton had the worst of it. He started second on soft slicks, had no grip at all on the wet track, and dropped down the order.

The race turns on lap 9

Valtteri Bottas slid off into the gravel on lap 9 and brought out the safety car. With the track drying, almost everyone dived into the pits for slicks. Antonelli went onto mediums and Verstappen onto softs.

That call won the race. Verstappen used the grippier tyre to get past Antonelli at the restart and never really looked back from there.

Hamilton's charge

The comeback of the day belonged to Hamilton. After plummeting at the start, he carved back up to fifth by lap 23. Ferrari pitted him for mediums on lap 31, then took advantage of the late neutralisations to bolt on fresh softs. He used them to pass Isack Hadjar in the closing laps and grab third.

His teammate Charles Leclerc had a quieter recovery story of his own, coming back from early trouble and a collision to finish fourth.

Chaos in the closing laps

With about ten laps left, Alex Albon went off, triggering a virtual safety car and then a full safety car. That shuffled the pit stops around at the front, and for a moment Antonelli was leading again before Mercedes brought him in.

Then came the big blow for Mercedes. Russell, running third, suffered a power failure while the safety car was out and parked his car. It's a costly zero in the title fight.

Verstappen handled the final restart and held off Antonelli to the flag.

My take: The formation lap fiasco demonstrated how awful the 2026 regulations are. Bring back the V8s!

What it means for the championship

Second place still did plenty for Antonelli. With Russell scoring nothing, the Mercedes rookie stretched his lead over his teammate to 84 points with seven races remaining.

There's also a question mark over how many of those races will actually run. The season-ending rounds in Qatar and Abu Dhabi are under threat because of the situation in the region, and one of them could be dropped.
