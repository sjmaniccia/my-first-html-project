Race Recap · Round 1 · March 8, 2026

Russell Leads a Mercedes One-Two to Open the New Era

The first race under the new rules turned into a battery-boost battle with Ferrari, and two virtual safety cars handed Mercedes the win.

The new era is officially underway, and it started with a familiar sight: a Mercedes on top. George Russell converted pole into a win in Melbourne, with teammate Kimi Antonelli right behind him for a one-two.

It wasn't as simple as that sounds, though. The opening laps gave us our first real look at what the 2026 rules mean for racing, and it was weird.

A battery-boost battle

Both Ferraris launched brilliantly and Russell got boxed in between them, while Antonelli briefly dropped to seventh. Russell and Charles Leclerc then traded the lead back and forth. Russell would use his electric boost to get by, drain his battery doing it, and Leclerc would boost straight back past. Welcome to 2026.

Two VSCs decide it

On lap 11, Isack Hadjar's Red Bull retired with an engine problem and brought out a virtual safety car. Mercedes pitted both cars. Ferrari kept theirs out, aiming for a one-stop.

It backfired. Russell came out in clean air and reeled the Ferraris in fast. Then on lap 16, Valtteri Bottas's Cadillac stopped with a fuel system problem and caused a second VSC. Russell was too close for Ferrari to pit without losing the lead, and the pit lane closed before either car could come in. By the time Ferrari stopped under green, Mercedes were first and second.

Antonelli cut the gap to Russell from six seconds to three on the hard tyres, but never got close enough to try a move. Leclerc held off Lewis Hamilton for the last podium spot.

Further back

Max Verstappen started 20th and drove up to sixth, with a long fight against Lando Norris along the way and the fastest lap of the race. Fernando Alonso passed seven cars early on, only to retire with vibrations.

It was a rough day before the race even started for two drivers. Oscar Piastri crashed on his way to the grid after an unexpected power surge, and Nico Hülkenberg's Audi didn't start because of a hydraulics problem.
