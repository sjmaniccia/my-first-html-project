Audi's First Season: Slow Start, Big Plans

A works team with its own power unit, a rough first half and signs of life since the summer. Audi's debut is all about 2030.

Team
Audi Revolut F1 Team
Base
Hinwil, Switzerland
Car
R26, with Audi’s own engine
Drivers
Nico Hülkenberg, Gabriel Bortoleto
Points
17, 8th in constructors
Best finish
8th (three times)

One of the biggest stories of 2026 has been happening in the midfield. Audi joined Formula 1 this year, not as an engine supplier for someone else, but as a full works team with its own chassis and its own power unit.

How Audi got here

Audi announced back in 2022 that it would enter F1 when the new engine rules arrived in 2026, and that the Sauber team would become its works squad. In 2024 it took full ownership of Sauber and brought in former Ferrari boss Mattia Binotto to lead the project.

The team is still based at Sauber's old home in Hinwil, Switzerland, and races as the Audi Revolut F1 Team, with Nico Hülkenberg and Gabriel Bortoleto driving the R26.

A rough first half

It started brilliantly. Bortoleto finished ninth in Melbourne and scored points on the team's debut, even though Hülkenberg's car didn't make the start. Then came a long run without scoring.

Reliability hurt. Bortoleto didn't start in China, and Hülkenberg had a miserable spell: overheating in Miami, and in Barcelona, gravel thrown up by Liam Lawson's car hit his safety switch and cut the car's electrics while he was running for ninth.

Signs of life

Upgrades before the summer break turned things around. Bortoleto finished eighth at both Silverstone and Spa, and Hülkenberg scored his first points with ninth in Hungary. Since the break, Hülkenberg added eighth at Zandvoort and tenth in Madrid, and Bortoleto says the Baku upgrade worked as planned.

That leaves Audi eighth in the constructors' championship with 17 points after 16 races. Bortoleto has been the stronger of the two on Sundays, but it's been close in qualifying.

Not everything has been smooth

There's been upheaval off track too. Team principal Jonathan Wheatley left during the season, and Binotto now runs the team as both team chief and team principal.

Audi has also been one of the louder voices in the engine debate. It joined F1 for these hybrid rules, and it opposed bringing in a new engine formula early.

The long game

Nobody at Audi expected to win straight away. The team calls 2026 and 2027 foundation years, and the stated goal is to win a world championship by 2030. Judged against that, a points finish on debut and a stronger second half is a decent start.

My take

F1 needed another engine manufacturer to bring competition and innovation to the sport after losing Renault. The sacrifices made to bring Audi in have been huge, and the team has been through a lot of growing pains. I wonder if the changes made to the sport are worth bringing in a midfield team that looks like it may not succeed.
