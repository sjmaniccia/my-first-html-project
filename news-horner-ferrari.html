Christian Horner to Ferrari? Here's What We Actually Know

Horner says Ferrari is his dream job, and Ferrari's season has gone flat. Here's what's fact, what's rumour, and what might happen next.

Every F1 season needs a rumour that won't go away, and this year's is Christian Horner at Ferrari. Nothing has been announced, and Ferrari hasn't said a word. But Horner himself has just poured fuel on the fire.

Why everyone is talking about it

In an interview with The Times around the Baku weekend, Horner said Ferrari is the only team he'd join without owning a stake in it. Until now, he'd made a shareholding a condition of his next job. He called Ferrari the most important brand in the sport, a sleeping giant, and said he doesn't need to go back to being a salaried employee anywhere else.

That's about as close as anyone in F1 gets to publicly applying for a job.

Horner's story so far

Horner ran Red Bull for 20 years, winning eight drivers' titles and six constructors' titles. Red Bull removed him as CEO and team principal after the 2025 British Grand Prix, with Laurent Mekies taking over, and a later exit agreement ended all his ties with the company.

Reports at the time said the payout was around $100 million, and that he could return to the grid by mid-2026. Since then he's been linked with Alpine, Haas and even starting his own team, but nothing has happened yet.

Ferrari's problem

Ferrari started the season brightly. Lewis Hamilton won in Barcelona, his first win in red, and Charles Leclerc followed it up at Silverstone. Since then, things have gone flat. Ferrari's power unit has fallen well behind Mercedes, and the other teams have caught up on the chassis side.

Ferrari is still second in the constructors' championship, but a long way behind Mercedes. And the pressure has landed on team principal Fred Vasseur. This year's SF-26 is the first Ferrari developed fully on his watch, so there's no blaming the previous regime.

So will it happen?

Honestly, nobody outside Maranello knows. Some analysts, like former team manager Peter Windsor, think Horner will end up there. Reports in Italy have suggested the talks are more than gossip, but none of it has been confirmed.

What's clear is that both pieces are in place: a team under pressure to change something, and the most successful team boss of the last two decades saying he wants the job. Whether Ferrari picks up the phone is another matter.
