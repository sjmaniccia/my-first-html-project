The 2026 Engines Aren't Working. Bring Back the V8s.

The new hybrid rules were supposed to be F1's future. Most of a season in, they've mostly given us battery management. The V8 plan can't come soon enough.

2026 engine
1.6L V6 turbo hybrid
Power split
About 50% engine, 50% battery
Reported next engine
3.0L turbo V8
Electric power
350kW now, ~100kW planned
Target date
2030, or 2031 at the latest

What changed this year

2026 brought the biggest rules shake-up in F1's history. The engines kept the 1.6-litre turbo V6, but the balance flipped. Electric power nearly tripled to 350kW, the MGU-H was dropped, and the cars now get roughly half their power from the battery. Everything runs on fully sustainable fuel.

On paper, that's the sport getting road-relevant and green at the same time. On track, it's been a different story.

What's gone wrong

Charging and deploying the battery has turned into the main event. Races are shaped by when drivers harvest energy and when they spend it, not just who's fastest through the corners. Drivers haven't held back, either. Max Verstappen has been so unhappy that he's openly questioned his future in the sport.

The FIA has already had to step in. After just three races, officials tweaked the rules to cut back how much the electric side decides things, especially in qualifying. When you're patching a brand-new formula three races in, something went wrong at the drawing board.

The V8s are (probably) coming back

Here's the good news. In May, FIA president Mohammed Ben Sulayem said a switch back to V8s with only minor electrification is a matter of time. His target is 2030, if enough manufacturers agree, and 2031 regardless.

Then in late September, reports said the FIA and the manufacturers had agreed on a framework: a 3.0-litre V8 with a smaller turbo, electric power cut from 350kW to around 100kW, and lighter cars. Nothing is official until it goes through the approval process, but it's the clearest sign yet that the V8 is really happening.

My take

These rules aren't great, and F1 should go back to V8s.

F1 is supposed to be about the best drivers pushing the fastest cars to the limit. A formula where the smart move is lifting off to recharge a battery isn't that. I watch to see who's bravest on the brakes, not who managed their energy best.

And then there's the sound. Anyone who heard the 2006 to 2013 cars in person knows. That scream was half the reason people fell in love with this sport. The hybrids have never come close.

A V8 is simpler, lighter and cheaper to build, and it would still run on sustainable fuel, so the green argument doesn't go away. My only worry is the reported plan keeps a turbo. If we're going back, let's go all the way.

2030 can't come soon enough.

The other side

To be fair, not everyone agrees. Manufacturers like Audi joined F1 under these hybrid rules, and an earlier push for V10s stalled when manufacturers objected. Some argue a V8 is a nostalgia trip that has little to do with the cars people actually drive. Others point out the 2026 rules are only one season old, and the tweaks are already helping.

Fair points. I just think the racing should come first.
