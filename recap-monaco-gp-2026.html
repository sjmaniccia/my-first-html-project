Race Recap · Round 6 · June 7, 2026

Antonelli Conquers Monaco With a Grand Slam

Pole, every lap led, the win and the fastest lap. Kimi Antonelli rewrote the record books on a chaotic afternoon in Monte Carlo.

Kimi Antonelli just became the youngest driver to win the Monaco Grand Prix, and he did it in style: pole, the win and the fastest lap, the youngest driver ever to pull off a grand slam. Five wins in a row.

For a track where overtaking is almost impossible, this race had plenty going on.

Verstappen out on lap 1

Antonelli kept the lead at the start. Behind him, Max Verstappen's power unit lost power, he dropped to the back, and he retired at the end of the first lap. Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc moved into second and third.

A pit lane speed trap

The field thinned out fast. Valtteri Bottas and Oliver Bearman retired with brake problems, and Lando Norris stopped with a loss of battery power.

Meanwhile, the stewards were handing out five-second penalties for pit lane speeding like parking tickets: Hamilton, George Russell, Pierre Gasly, Franco Colapinto and Oscar Piastri all got one.

Safety car, then a red flag

With 22 laps left, Lance Stroll crashed at the final corner, bringing out the safety car. Several drivers used it to pit and serve their penalties. When racing resumed, Leclerc crashed at the very same corner, and the race was red-flagged.

Antonelli kept the lead at the standing restart. Russell briefly got ahead before having to serve a drive-through, which dropped him out of the points. Carlos Sainz's race ended after collisions with Nico Hülkenberg and Colapinto.

The podium that changed hands

Gasly crossed the line third, but his two pit lane penalties dropped him to seventh, which handed Isack Hadjar his first podium of the season.

The saga didn't end there. Alpine won a right of review that put Gasly back on the podium, but in September, the FIA's International Court of Appeal reversed it, so Gasly is back to seventh. A post-race penalty for Sergio Pérez also bumped Fernando Alonso into tenth, giving Aston Martin its first point of the year.
